Project Sniper

Start here

A video editor with one setup package for Apple silicon, Intel Mac and Windows x64. You describe the result in plain language; it cuts, captions, adds graphics and renders a finished video — and gives you the editable project behind it.

This copy is a release candidate, not a finished product. It has not passed every release qualification test, its full-length and Short output tests, or its delivery test. The licence terms in this package are a draft and are not in force. Read RELEASE.json for this build's exact status, and PENDING-OWNER-DECISIONS.txt for what is still unsettled.

Three steps

  1. Check what you have. A Mac with Apple silicon and macOS 13.5 or newer, an Intel Mac with macOS 14 or newer, or a 64-bit Windows PC on Windows 10 22H2 (build 19045) or newer; an internet connection for the first setup; about 4 GB for Sniper and its tools plus at least 20 GB free while rendering (the checks refuse less), and your own Codex (ChatGPT plan) or Claude Code (Claude plan), signed in as you already use it. You do not install Node, Python, ffmpeg or anything else yourself: Sniper downloads and checks its own. The native Intel installer is exercised on a macOS 15 Intel runner, and the Windows x64 installer is exercised on a clean Windows Server 2025 runner. The full list, with what costs money, is in What Sniper is.
  2. Open this folder in Codex or Claude Code and say “Set up Sniper”. Your agent installs Sniper's tools (several minutes, mostly downloads), asking your permission as it goes, and checks the result. There is nothing to sign in to and no app to open: you talk to Sniper through your own agent, on your own subscription. Sniper keeps its tools in ~/.project-sniper on macOS or %LOCALAPPDATA%\ProjectSniper on Windows and everything else, including your video projects (in projects), inside this folder; your own Node, Python, Homebrew, Codex or Claude setup and login are not used or changed. You can also double-click install/setup.command on macOS or run sniper.cmd setup on Windows. Step-by-step: Install and first use.
  3. Make your first edit. Ask your agent. Your first three edits walks a short test clip you record yourself, then a full-length video, then a Short from your own recording.
