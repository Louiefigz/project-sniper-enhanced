**Lesson:** Repeated customer questions and unanswered calls consume attention; the useful product story should show a relevant inquiry becoming an answer, booking or handoff, then explain the human benefit.

**Opening:** An unanswered-call scenario is paired with a negative colour cue, then a competitor consequence. This gives the viewer a recognisable business cost before the personal event example.

**Story:** Missed call → competitor risk → personal virtual event → underestimated question volume → impatient customers → receptionist introduction → question/answer and channel examples → booking/FAQ/routing claims → busy-team fallback → language claim → worried-versus-focused owner contrast → branded trial CTA.

**Payoff:** The short makes the workload and desired benefit concrete and shows several recognisable interface examples. It does not prove the numerical booking count, automatic answering, multilingual fluency or customer outcomes; some example screens do not match the current spoken claim.

**Why study this case:** A dense full-length mix of human problem scenes, split product orientation, progressive question cards, one local avatar selection, a targeted call-control pointer, numeric emphasis, short demonstrations and a human outcome contrast. It distinguishes new pictures from actual action/result evidence.
