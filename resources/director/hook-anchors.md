# Director opening patterns (hook anchors)

An anchor is a reusable pattern for a Short's written opening. Its bracketed
slots are filled with words the speaker actually said in the retained cut.
Authored for Project Sniper on 2026-09-18 (see `README.md`).

Categories are named for the **source material that powers them**. Choose a
category only after finding that material in the retained recording; a pattern
whose material is missing cannot be rescued with generic topic words. Inside a
category, pick the anchor whose notes match the recording, then fill every slot.

Quoted templates can be filled literally by the local title tool. One clause is
optional: in `steps-toward-goal`, drop ` (in [timeframe])` when no timeframe is
spoken. The notes column says what the recording must contain (`Needs`) and when
the pattern misleads (`Avoid`).

### 1. NAMED SITUATION — the viewer recognises their own situation in the first line

| Anchor | Template | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `situation-if-you` | "If you [situation], [consequence]" | Needs: a situation the target viewer is in and the consequence the speaker attaches to it. Avoid: a consequence the Director inferred. |
| `situation-for-anyone-whose` | "For anyone whose [thing] [symptom]" | Needs: an observable symptom the speaker describes. Avoid: audiences named only by job title or ambition. |
| `situation-this-is-why` | "[situation]? This is why" | Needs: a frustration the speaker names and explains within the retained cut. Avoid: when the cause is only hinted. |
| `situation-before-you` | "Before you [action], check [thing]" | Needs: an action the viewer is about to take and one concrete check the speaker names. Avoid: checks that are not explained. |

### 2. OPEN QUESTION — a precise question that the retained recording answers

| Anchor | Template | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `question-cause-behind` | "Why does [thing] [observation]?" | Needs: something the viewer has noticed and the reason the speaker gives. Avoid: an observation without a stated cause. |
| `question-what-happens-when` | "What happens when you [action]?" | Needs: an action and its result, shown or described. Avoid: a result the footage does not show and the speaker does not state. |
| `question-how-much` | "How much [quantity] does [thing] take?" | Needs: a spoken quantity with its unit. Avoid: questions whose answer is an estimate the speaker did not give. |
| `question-what-is-missing` | "What is missing from [artifact]?" | Needs: one missing element the speaker identifies. Avoid: several missing elements (use a counted-steps anchor). |

### 3. COUNTED STEPS — the speaker walks through an explicit sequence

| Anchor | Template | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `steps-toward-goal` | "[count] steps to [goal] (in [timeframe])" | Needs: steps the speaker counts and a goal the speaker names. The timeframe clause is optional and used only when a timeframe is spoken. Avoid: counting steps the speaker did not count. |
| `steps-order-matters` | "[first step] before you [second step]" | Needs: two steps and the speaker's reason the order matters. Avoid: an order stated without a reason. |
| `steps-first-of` | "Step one of [task]: [first step]" | Needs: a procedure whose first step is concrete and whose remaining steps stay in the cut. Avoid: a cut that ends after step one. |
| `steps-checks-before` | "[count] checks before you [action]" | Needs: a counted list of checks. Avoid: fewer than two checks in the retained cut. |

### 4. REAL NUMBER — a number with its unit that the speaker states

| Anchor | Template | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `number-adds-up` | "[small amount] a [period] is [total] a [longer period]" | Needs: both amounts spoken with the same unit family. Avoid: a total computed by the Director or rounding the speaker did not do. |
| `number-here-is-what` | "[number] [term]: here is what that means" | Needs: a number the viewer has seen and the speaker's interpretation. Avoid: an interpretation that becomes a promise. |
| `number-took-not` | "It took [actual], not [expected]" | Needs: both the actual and the expected figure in the speaker's words. Avoid: an expectation attributed to the viewer without support. |
| `number-the-one` | "The number to watch: [figure]" | Needs: a figure the speaker singles out and explains. Avoid: presenting a goal or projection as an achieved result. |

### 5. COSTLY MISTAKE — a mistake and what it costs the viewer

| Anchor | Template | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `mistake-you-might-be` | "You might be [mistake]" | Needs: a common mistake and its correction in the retained cut. Avoid: a mistake without a fix. |
| `mistake-can-cost` | "[mistake] can cost you [cost]" | Needs: the cost stated by the speaker. Avoid: costs inferred by the Director or stated as certain when the speaker hedged. |
| `mistake-called-common` | "A common [task] mistake: [mistake]" | Needs: the speaker calls it common or frequent. Avoid: frequency claims the speaker did not make. |
| `mistake-looks-right` | "[thing] looks right but [problem]" | Needs: something that appears correct and the hidden problem the speaker explains. Avoid: problems that are asserted but not explained. |

### 6. ASSUMPTION CHECK — a common belief that the recording tests

| Anchor | Template | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `assumption-no-need` | "You don't need [assumed requirement] for [goal]" | Needs: the speaker says the requirement is unnecessary and names the alternative. Avoid: widening the scope beyond the speaker's case. |
| `assumption-half-true` | "[belief]? Only half true" | Needs: the speaker says which part of the belief holds and which does not. Avoid: beliefs nobody in the audience holds. |
| `assumption-more-wont` | "More [thing] won't fix [problem]" | Needs: the speaker argues that quantity is not the fix and names what is. Avoid: when no alternative fix is given. |
| `assumption-except-when` | "[common advice], except when [exception]" | Needs: an exception the speaker names with its reason. Avoid: exceptions the Director supplies. |

### 7. SIDE BY SIDE — two options or two versions compared

| Anchor | Template | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `compare-two-options` | "[first option] or [second option] for [purpose]?" | Needs: two options compared on points the speaker states. Avoid: a comparison with a single point. |
| `compare-same-task` | "Same [thing], two [variants]" | Needs: both versions shown or described in the recording. Avoid: an invented second version. |
| `compare-before-after` | "[thing] before and after [change]" | Needs: both states shown or described. Avoid: an after-state that is a hope rather than an observation. |
| `compare-when-each` | "When to use [first option], when to use [second option]" | Needs: a condition for each option in the speaker's words. Avoid: conditions for only one option. |

### 8. MOMENT FROM THE STORY — a specific event that the speaker lived

| Anchor | Template | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `moment-the-day` | "The day [event]" | Needs: a specific event with at least one concrete detail. Avoid: generic anecdotes. |
| `moment-i-noticed` | "I noticed [observation] when I [action]" | Needs: the speaker's own first-person observation. Avoid: turning an observation into a general rule. |
| `moment-what-changed` | "What changed after I [action]" | Needs: the change described by the speaker. Avoid: measured results the speaker did not measure. |
| `moment-first-time` | "The first time I [action], [what happened]" | Needs: both the action and what happened, spoken. Avoid: a lesson that does not follow from the moment. |

### 9. PLAIN DEFINITION — a term or idea that the speaker explains

| Anchor | Template | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `define-what-means` | "What [term] actually means" | Needs: a definition in the retained cut. Avoid: a term the speaker only uses. |
| `define-one-sentence` | "[term], in one sentence" | Needs: a one-sentence definition from the speaker. Avoid: definitions stitched from separate sentences. |
| `define-is-not` | "[term] is not [misreading]" | Needs: the speaker distinguishes the term from a common misreading. Avoid: misreadings the Director supplies. |
| `define-works-like` | "[idea] works like [familiar object]" | Needs: an analogy that is the speaker's own. Avoid: analogies that import a factual claim. |

### 10. VISIBLE RESULT — an operation or result that is visible in the footage

| Anchor | Template | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `result-watch-the` | "Watch the [thing] when I [action]" | Needs: footage in which the change is visible on the spoken cue. Avoid: changes too small to read at phone size. |
| `result-this-is-what` | "This is what [action] does to [thing]" | Needs: visible before and after states in the footage. Avoid: states that are only described. |
| `result-in-duration` | "[task] in [duration]" | Needs: the operation takes the stated duration in real time. Avoid: sped-up or trimmed footage. |
| `result-where-to-find` | "Where to find [setting] in [tool]" | Needs: a screen recording in which the setting is visible and legible. Avoid: interfaces that are not in the material. |
