**Lesson:** A location-aware day planner should reason about specific stops, distances, routes and ordering instead of offering an unconnected list; the visual presentation should make those relationships inspectable.

**Opening:** The spoken novelty is Claude gaining location awareness through MapQuest MCP. Familiar logos identify the combination, then route alternatives provide a visible preview of its promise. It is a capability reveal, not a separate contrarian title card.

**Story:** Name the integration → preview route output → unpack nearby/distance/time/traffic with matching capability visuals → establish one-day New York scenario → display a cumulative prompt → reject random recommendations → illustrate places, routes, ordering and a five-minute trip → show destination experience → broaden to other agents → offer access and keyword CTA.

**Payoff:** The viewer sees how spatial relationships can make a day plan useful and sees the shape of relevant inputs/results. A single continuous executed New York itinerary is not demonstrated: city/rendition changes and generic stop lists leave that proof unclosed.

**Why study this case:** A complete example of the same split host supporting many distinct informational jobs, a justified 4.5-second input hold, UI-detail enlargement, destination B-roll, and a digit-plus-unit incentive. It also exposes the risk of visually plausible but task-inconsistent map assets.

**Review limits:** Viewed all 5 chronological 0.5-second context pages (90 samples including final frame) plus 90 framing samples on 10 extra strips. Original machine-caption grouping supplies spoken ideas; no audio audition or phonetic verification. Caption final word overruns video; source was not padded or retimed. Strip timestamps are ffmpeg eight-fps resampling estimates, not source-frame measurements.
