Reviewing the video, and opening the editable project

Every finished edit gives you two things: the video, and the project behind its graphics layer. Check both.

1. Watch the video

projects/<project>/producer/final.mp4

Open it and watch the whole thing with the sound on. Not a scrub, not a screenshot, not a look at the first ten seconds. The defects that matter — a clipped word at a join, a caption that lags, a graphic that leaves before it is readable — only show up in playback.

The checklist that catches real defects

  1. Does the opening make sense on its own, and does the ending answer it?
  2. Is any word cut off at a join?
  3. Do the captions match the words that are actually spoken?
  4. Is every number and claim on screen something you really said?
  5. Is the framing sensible in every shot, including where you moved?
  6. Is the audio intelligible, and not harsh or pumping?
  7. Does the motion stop when the content needs to be read?

2. Open the editable project

The graphics layer is a real editable project, not a flattened picture. This is the manual-control surface:

install/studio.command open projects/<project>/producer

That builds the review project from the edit plan plus the graphics-free video, and opens it in HyperFrames Studio in your browser. You move panels, change timing, adjust what a card says. Always go through install/studio.command: it runs Studio with this install's own settings (its Node, browser and ffmpeg, and none of the API keys in your shell), which running the Python environment directly would skip.

Studio is a review and control surface. It previews; it does not render your deliverable. Your footage is never re-encoded in a browser.

Folding your Studio edits back in

install/studio.command sync projects/<project>/producer --apply

This writes your changes into the edit plan, after checking them and taking a backup. It then prints the exact rebuild command to run, which is:

install/studio.command rebuild projects/<project>/producer

Run it to rebuild the video; only the stages your change invalidated are redone.

# see where the lane currently is and what to do next
install/studio.command status projects/<project>/producer

# shut the preview server down when you are finished
install/studio.command stop projects/<project>/producer

Two things to know. First, syncing is not approval — after your graphics change, the checks and reviews run again before the result counts as finished. Second, Sniper refuses to overwrite Studio edits you have not synced. If you see that refusal, sync first. There is a --force flag that discards them; do not use it unless you mean to throw that work away.

3. Both views must agree

After any adjustment, the video and the project should show the same content. If you changed graphics in Studio but did not rebuild, they disagree — and the video is the old one. Rebuild, then watch it again.

Where everything lives, and what to keep

PathWhat it isKeep?
source/Your footage and its transcriptsYes — this is your original material
producer/edit_plan.jsonThe edit itself, as dataYes — this is the edit; it can rebuild the video
producer/final.mp4The approved videoYes
producer/base_final.mp4The video without graphicsOptional; speeds up rebuilds
Render cachesRegenerable working filesNo — install/clean-caches.command lists and removes only these, and the kept downloads of Sniper's own tools (about 290 MB; without them, repairing the tools downloads them again)

Back up your projects folder (or the folder you chose at setup). That is the whole of your work; everything else in the Sniper folder is replaceable.
