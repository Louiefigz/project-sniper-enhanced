# Choose a format from the viewer's need

Version `2026-09-10.3`. Start here when directing a Short. The references teach
why a picture can help a particular message, how to organize it, and when its
job ends. Adapt those relationships to our footage, words and assets. Creator
names help locate examples; they do not select a whole-video preset.

The decision chain is **message → viewing need → format → framing → development
and readable hold → exit condition**. The explanations of purpose below are
editorial interpretations of observed sequences, not statements from their editors
or evidence of a retention advantage.

## A split screen has several distinct purposes

**Relationship plus explanation.** CR07's upper pane holds concepts and connections;
the lower pane holds the person explaining them. The diagram reduces what the
viewer must remember, while his hand gestures echo combining and branching.
The diagram develops inside a stable format: adding a node, retaining the idea,
growing branches, changing labels. Each new fact does not require a new layout.

At roughly 18.6s the relationship has been shown and the next passage qualifies
what the advice means. The video returns to a large portrait. In the inspected
native burst, the split geometry is changing at 18.519s and the first sampled
full portrait is at 18.602s. This supports an earned exit tied to the new thought;
it does not prescribe an 18.6-second split for another script.

**Artifact plus explanation.** LM06's screen area carries the current object or

on a particular row, focus and hold that row, or give it the whole frame.

**Comparison.** N03's output and narrator establish a different relationship.
Its two views are not interchangeable with a diagram/presenter split. Retain
the corresponding identities, comparable detail and the moment the viewer should
inspect. If sound is part of the claimed comparison, visual review alone cannot
qualify it.

### What a well-executed split must do

Write the job of each pane in a sentence, then draw its actual geometry. A job
description alone does not guarantee a useful composition.

- Give the explanatory object enough space for its decisive detail to be read
  during the available speech. Simplify labels or focus a capture before shrinking it.
- Frame the presenter deliberately inside the remaining area. Preserve the face
  and meaningful gestures. A landscape image contained as a tiny rectangle inside
  a portrait pane wastes the space; inspect a crop against the actual source.
- Establish a primary place to look on each cue. Let the speaker orient the
  viewer, then make the new node, selected field or changed result prominent.
  Avoid simultaneous competing entrances in both panes.
- Place captions where they remain readable without covering diagram labels,
  critical controls, eyes or hands. Captions do not automatically need a detached
  empty panel. Geometry depends on the footage and the explanatory content.
- Keep the same object identifiable across input, action and result. A new
  screenshot from a different example can break the demonstrated claim.
- Remove, enlarge or replace a pane when its job changes. A static decorative
  diagram beside a complete personal argument has no automatic entitlement to half the frame.

CR07's big central concept, short branch labels and visible hands are useful
relationships to transfer. Its yellow circles, texture, exact split ratio and six
branches are choices for that source. Our diagram needs the number of branches
and emphasis justified by our explanation, using our readable font system.

## Decide what develops before deciding what transitions

There are several kinds of change. Keep them distinct in the plan:

| Change | Source example | Decision for a new Short |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Information develops inside the same composition. | CR07 adds a solution, produces an idea, then branches it. | Preserve object identity and spatial memory. Reveal on the new explanation's cue; hold the result. |
| A brief marker organizes ongoing delivery. | CR06 replaces ordinary captions briefly with the next question number. | Mark the new section, then return attention to the actual question. Do not stack redundant text systems. |
| A title has completed its job. | CR04's opening promise clears around 5.2s; LM07's context heading clears around 6s. | Remove the heading when the viewer is oriented. A category heading can remain longer if it still organizes the lesson. |
| The operation needs another artifact. | LM06 changes from test results to the website headline. | Show what is carried forward and why. Keep the winning input and changed output traceable. |
| The viewing need changes. | CR07 returns from relationships to qualification; LM06 returns from diagnostic to presenter. | Change format at that thought, not at an arbitrary elapsed-time quota. |
| Footage changes while the sentence remains useful. | LM05 holds a text passage across several personal views. | Keep the narrative legible across cuts. Decide whether the Short itself answers the opening question. |

A thoughtful delivery, a changing diagram and an actively edited screen can each
earn a long hold. A motionless generic illustration cannot earn that hold merely
because another creator kept a shot for a similar duration. Record the new
information, performance or inspection opportunity sustaining attention.

## Fonts, text and reading hierarchy

Separate **opening promise**, **speech captions**, **category/step marker**,
**diagram labels** and **evidence text**. Each has a different lifetime and job.
Use Aaron's preference for clean, readable Google Fonts; avoid condensed headings
and typewriter-style body text. The reference's appearance does not identify its
font family, and a catalog default does not choose our typography.

Select and bind an explicit family, weight, line height, width and placement for
the actual project. Use one coherent hierarchy across changes of format. Verify
the loaded font in the rendered project; do not approve a fallback font by accident.
Emphasis should identify a meaningful contrast or key phrase. Keep ordinary
captions tied to the retained speech and protect their phrase boundaries.

CR06's brief ordinal demonstrates hierarchy and timing without requiring gold
serif captions. CR05 demonstrates a pause-and-read card but is too dense to adopt
unchanged as a simultaneous spoken lesson. LM04's checklist, physical card and
large captions sometimes compete. Preserve the organizing idea and improve the
reading load where our message needs it. Judge at phone size and real playback
speed, including the fully revealed state and the transition into the next text.

## B-roll and visual representations follow the claim

For each proposed visual, name its role: **evidence, explanation, context, or
attention support**. Then bind an actual inspected asset or explicit diagram input.

| Role | Acquisition and directing choice |
| --- | --- |
| Evidence of a product/action/result | Reuse the matching long-form capture or asset when it proves this exact point. Otherwise capture the real operation and result. Preserve source identity and relevant values. A browser shell supplies presentation, not proof. |
| Explanation of a relationship | Author an original diagram from the actual supported entities and relationships. Search the primary HyperFrames registry for the mechanism, then configure or compose it. The diagram explains a claim; it does not independently verify it. |
| Personal or situational context | Prefer relevant footage from the source or available asset pool. Obtain suitable licensed material when needed and record its source. A travel montage may situate a personal story but does not prove a revenue claim. |
| Tangible offer | Show the actual resource and a representative useful portion. Do not insert a generic document and imply it is the promised deliverable. |
| Attention support | Preserve useful gestures, physical props or movement. LM04's food handling adds activity; it is not evidence about ad performance. Avoid B-roll selected only because it matches a noun in the transcript. |

LM06's visible highest metric row and its later replacement headline do not
clearly establish the same winner. Treat that as a source limitation. Our
adaptation must make the input → selected result → applied change consistent.
Generated imagery can explain or contextualize when appropriate; it cannot stand
in for a real product result or documentary proof.

## Use the library before assembly

This is reference context for agent planning, not model fine-tuning. Read the
actual project brief, selected source speech and available assets first. Retrieve
examples by viewing need; inspect the complete selected case, its individual
frames and at least one plausible contrasting choice. Document this compact
decision in the existing strategy/storyboard:

1. **Message and payoff:** what this retained passage actually teaches, including
   its qualification; which words and source range deliver it.
2. **Viewing need and choice:** what the viewer needs to see, selected format,
   pane jobs and why a plausible alternate is weaker for this passage.
3. **Framing and text:** source crop, pane rectangles, face/hand clearance,
   decisive detail size, text roles and reading order.
4. **Development and exit:** input/state → visible action → result → inspection
   hold; speech cue for each event and the condition for changing format.
5. **Evidence and implementation:** reference case/beat/version/frame paths,
   inspected real assets, primary HyperFrames catalog item and source hash,
   configuration/composition required and any unresolved gap.
6. **Checks on the candidate:** what a reviewer must be able to read, follow and
   conclude at phone size; source-truth, caption, crop and audiovisual checks.

Follow the existing [prebuild strategy checkpoint](../../producer/NATIVE_PREBUILD_STRATEGY_2026-09-10.md)
before HTML assembly. A fresh critic should challenge the format and geometry
against the actual brief and source. That checkpoint is an internal review, not
an additional user approval round. A rejected split should prompt a better
message-based decision; it must not create a global presenter-only fallback.

Use the **hosted HyperFrames registry as the primary catalog**, reusing the
long-form discovery, asset and Studio paths. [Inspected candidates and gaps](authentic-expansion/CATALOG_MAP.md)
show how to translate the decision into mechanisms. The legacy local item count
is not a creative limit or a native Shorts readiness test.

Inspect the adapted sequence's entrance, readable state and exit, then play it
with audio at phone size. Check whether it delivers its promise, not merely
whether its code renders. Keep the actual native source editable. These written
instructions do not by themselves implement automatic retrieval or prove the
end-to-end Shorts workflow; see the [readiness audit](../../producer/SHORTS_READINESS_AUDIT_2026-09-10.md).

## Evidence scope

This guide draws on the existing Nate and six-reel contrast studies plus
[eight additional complete Caleb/Lewis cases](authentic-expansion/README.md).
The expansion has 39 directing beats, 541 chronological 2 fps samples, 213 unique
selected native frames and 13 inspected motion bursts. Individual-frame checks,
transcript availability and limitations are recorded in [QA](authentic-expansion/QA.md).
No source audio audition, uninterrupted playback, exact font identification,
retention causality or rendered adaptation qualification is claimed by this study.
