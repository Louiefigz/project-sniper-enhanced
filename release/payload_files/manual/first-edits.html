Your first three edits

A short test clip you record, then a full-length video, then a vertical Short from your own recording. Do them in this order.

Two ways to drive it — pick one

By asking (the main way). Open your editor brain in the app folder and describe what you want. Sniper's editing instructions live in that folder, so it picks them up automatically.

install/editor.command

That opens the subscription you chose, inside the app folder, using this package's own sign-in and settings.

By clicking. Run install/start.command and use the page at http://127.0.0.1:3000. The page is optional — it prepares the same request the conversation does.

Every video is checked first. Before any edit reads a file, Producer copies it into the project and decodes it in a macOS sandbox. install/doctor.command shows this working on its media admission (real sample) line; if that line fails, fix it before your first edit.

There is no sample clip in this candidate. No footage has been cleared for redistribution, so nothing is shipped rather than shipping something Sniper does not have the rights to. Record your own test clip instead — it takes two minutes.

Before edit one — record a test clip

Use your phone or your Mac's camera. Sit facing the camera, and read the script below naturally, including the one deliberate mistake: say the marked line wrong, pause, then say it again correctly. Leave a two-second silence where it says so. That gives the first edit something real to remove.

Hi, this is my first test of Project Sniper.
I'm recording this to check that my install works from start to finish.
[pause for two seconds]
The goal today is simple: remove the silences, and the take I get wrong.
Here's the line I'll get wrong on purpose — the quick brown fox jumps over the lazy cat.
[pause]
The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog.
If the finished clip has no long pauses and only the second version of that line,
the edit worked. That's the end of the test.

Save it as ~/Movies/test-clip.mp4 (or change the path in the request below). About 45 seconds is right.

Edit one — the smallest possible thing

The point is to prove the machinery works end to end before you spend an hour on a real video. Ask for the cheapest possible edit: no graphics, no music, no b-roll.

Trim-only clean cut of ~/Movies/test-clip.mp4 — remove the silences
and bad takes, nothing else. No graphics, no music, no captions.

What should happen: it transcribes locally, proposes the cuts, checks them, renders, inspects the result, and gives you a path. Where the result is:

~/ProjectSniper/test-clip-<date>/producer/final.mp4

Review it: open it and watch the whole thing with the sound on. The two-second pause should be gone, and only the correct "lazy dog" line should remain. Listen for a word clipped at a cut. That is the one defect a trim-only edit can introduce, and it is the thing you are checking for.

If this works, the install is good. If it does not, run install/doctor.command before trying anything larger.

Edit two — a full-length video, with the lanes you choose

Full-length means 16:9. There are no styles here; you pick which production lanes you want on. Ask for the ones you want by name:

Edit ~/Movies/lesson.mp4 as a full-length 16:9 video.
Turn on: cuts, captions, motion, graphics, transitions, audio cleanup.
Leave b-roll and music off. Keep every claim on screen matched to what
I actually said.

An unchecked lane stays out of scope — it is not quietly added. A lane you asked for still has to pass its own checks, and if one cannot run you are told which and why instead of getting a silently thinner edit.

Start with a real but short one. A ten-minute video is a reasonable first full-length attempt. Expect this to take a while; see what to expect.

Review it properly: watch the whole export start to finish with audio, then open the editable project — both views, not just one.

Edit three — a vertical Short from your own recording

Shorts are 9:16 and are where the styles live. You can describe the treatment you want, or let it choose after looking at your footage.

Make the strongest Short from ~/Movies/lesson.mp4.
Find one standalone idea that makes sense on its own — a clear opening,
real evidence in the middle, and an ending that answers the opening.
Presenter-led, close crop, readable centred captions.
Keep it under 90 seconds.

Other requests that work, because they are things the editor actually reasons about:

  • "Choose the best treatment from this recording automatically."
  • "The best 3 Shorts from this recording" — it compares moments across the whole source rather than slicing it up by topic.
  • "Show the formula developing, then hold the finished result long enough to read it."
  • "When I mention a product, show its real website scrolled to that feature."

Check these five things on the finished Short, because they are the defects that actually happen:

  1. It says one complete thought. It does not start mid-sentence or stop mid-idea.
  2. No word is clipped at a cut.
  3. The captions match the words that survived, and are readable at phone size.
  4. You are framed sensibly the whole way through, including any shot where you moved.
  5. The audio is intelligible and not harsh.
