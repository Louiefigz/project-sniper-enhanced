**Lesson:** The proposed workflow directs a project through text, story and character objects rather than stopping at one clip; project timeline context is the clearest evidence of the multi-shot unit.

**Opening:** Output beauty, street and action shots above the presenter build appeal, then OpenArt identity and a directing metaphor lead to the Vibe Directing name.

**Story:** Output appeal → category name → one-clip/whole-sequence comparison → prompt/story/character/pacing surfaces → coherent action sequence and lifestyle continuity examples → multi-clip editor preview → interpretation and creator relevance → product/link CTA.

**Payoff:** The source makes the project-level idea concrete with planning objects and a timeline around related shots. It does not show a complete requested revision propagating, verify one-run/five-minute generation, or prove stable voices. Character/style examples are suggestive visual evidence with provenance limits.

**Why study this case:** Separates generated-footage movement from editorial zoom; includes a horizontal character-reference pan, sequence continuity through retained subjects, and the difference between output spectacle and a visible project container.

**Review limits:** All four chronological 0.5s sheets (65 samples including final frame) were opened, plus five selected 6fps seek-based strips. This is complete-duration sampled visual coverage, not every-source-frame review. No audio listening; speech meaning comes from timed machine captions and visible captions. Strip timestamps are approximate requested/fps-filter times, not measured source-frame PTS or exact cut boundaries. Footage provenance, voice consistency, generation duration and easing are unverified; VTT tail overrun is excluded from playable timing claims. Final delivery QA inspected native-PTS character-sheet captures and corrected an earlier face-zoom interpretation to a stable-scale lateral pan.
