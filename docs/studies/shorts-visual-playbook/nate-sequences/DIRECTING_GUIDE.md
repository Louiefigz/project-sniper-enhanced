# Choose the picture by what the viewer needs to understand

This guide links several actual examples for each directing decision. Use it with the full cases and their images; a case title or a catalog item name cannot convey the sequence by itself. The descriptions distinguish visible action from an inferred purpose. They do not establish why a creator made the choice or whether it improved retention.

## Start with the story, then choose the treatment

Write the retained lesson in one sentence. Identify what the viewer initially misunderstands, the example that changes that understanding, and the useful answer at the end. Use the existing YouTube title formulas to develop supported hook candidates around that uncertainty. Pair the selected hook with the actual opening speech and a picture that begins the same story. A product logo names the subject; it rarely supplies the whole reason to stay.

The [student-kit foundation](../../../../artifacts/nate-herk-30-shorts-study-2026-09-10/STUDENT_KIT_FOUNDATION.md) supplies this order. The cases supply concrete directing evidence. Choose an authentic or more produced treatment from the source performance and the explanatory burden. A persuasive personal judgment can remain on the person; a process, comparison or spatial relationship often needs a visible example.

## A zoom needs a target and an information job

The deck example is a useful enlargement: the small preview becomes a readable slide, and the presenter remains available below it. The move changes what the viewer can inspect. [N10 B19–20](cases/N10.md#N10-B19).

![Small result becomes inspectable](/Users/aaronfigueroa/development/demos/YT-Automation/PROJECT_SNIPER/docs/studies/shorts-visual-playbook/nate-sequences/sequences/N10-B19.jpg)

The calendar and website examples isolate a relevant area largely through a spotlight. The avatar example selects and enlarges one member of a group. The taxi example shrinks one object and grows its replacement inside an unchanged monitor. These are different mechanisms, despite all changing visual emphasis. [N04 B13](cases/N04.md#N04-B13), [N02 B24](cases/N02.md#N02-B24), [N05 B16](cases/N05.md#N05-B16), [N26 B06](cases/N26.md#N26-B06).

A closer presenter crop can emphasize an objection or conclusion without revealing new information. Label it as emphasis. Do not invent an explanatory zoom rationale or infer a continuous camera move merely because two sampled crops differ. A number counting up and a bar shortening are object animations, not camera movement.

For a new plan, record the initial target, final target, reason for the move, speech cue, move duration and readable hold. The inspected `ui-focus-zoom` works on a supplied capture and explicit anchor; it does not discover the target or track the speaker automatically. Use native video crop/keyframes when that is the simpler truthful mechanism.

## Show the change, then let it be understood

In the contractor analogy, the offer retains its value while a time bar appears at full length and contracts. That visible state change carries the meaning of a shorter duration. The subsequent reply remains in the same conversation, so it has a visible cause. [N27 B15–17](cases/N27.md#N27-B15).

![Time advantage is shown through changing length](/Users/aaronfigueroa/development/demos/YT-Automation/PROJECT_SNIPER/docs/studies/shorts-visual-playbook/nate-sequences/sequences/N27-B16.jpg)

For Aaron's example of video editing going through five steps, first establish the five actual steps from the script. Keep the same recognizable clip or project moving through five observable changes. If the script names selection, trimming, audio, graphics and export, those could become selected source ranges, a shortened timeline, an audio change, the actual overlay appearing and the finished output. Those states are an illustrative planning example; substitute the real named steps. Five boxes containing the step names would mainly repeat the narration.

Before building, establish which states can be shown with real screen capture, which have a matching catalog mechanism, and which need a bounded composition of existing parts. Obtain the clip thumbnails, timeline/capture states, waveform or graphic assets that the chosen depiction actually needs. Do not discover these missing inputs after assembling the whole Short.

## Preserve pacing without forcing constant layout changes

The taxi scene has a quick succession of alternatives, then the person gets roughly five seconds to explain the point. The contractor case has roughly 6.5 seconds of presenter interpretation after the illustrated offer. The voice-input case retains one interface while the request grows. These are different kinds of useful time.

Plan each beat as orientation → action or change → readable result → handoff. Shorten redundant establishment, empty waiting and already-understood examples. Preserve the time required to read a result, compare alternatives or understand an argument. Distinguish scene cuts, internal information changes, camera changes, object motion and captions in the pacing notes. A beat count is an annotation count, not a template count or a cuts-per-minute score.

Use a split when the viewer benefits from both the explanation and its visual example. Name what each pane contributes. Use full visual when reading, comparison or action needs the whole portrait. Keep the person full frame for relevant experience, stakes, conviction, interpretation or a conversational pivot. An inset is useful only when it guides without covering the action.

## Choose the evidence and the mechanism before production

1. Select a complete reference sequence for the same story job and inspect at least one alternative from this guide. Open the individual full frames for type, labels or small controls; use strips for continuity. Record the chosen beat IDs and why the alternative is less suitable.
2. Decide the required asset: actual product output, website capture, owned B-roll, or a clearly illustrative diagram. A logo names a product, a screenshot can show a control, and a result can show what happened; they supply different evidence.
3. Inspect the exact catalog source and its slots/variables. Prefer reuse, configuration and composition. Record any missing relationship or state change before bounded custom work. The [catalog map](CATALOG_MAP.md) records partial candidates, not verified replicas.
4. Specify new speech cues, active change and reading/performance holds. Carry a visible object or context across shots where the story depends on causality. Do not copy the reference's timestamps onto different speech.
5. Compare the assembled Studio opening, key transitions and payoff with those same references at phone size. Hide captions briefly: does the central visual relationship still make sense? Restore captions and verify that the picture and words agree.

The reference study is now more detailed, but it is still research. Exact audio synchronization needs source audio review; candidate adaptations need an actual Studio check. These files strengthen planning and comparison; they do not implement automatic retrieval or certify production quality.
