# CR04 · Explain an exercise through the conversation

Version `2026-09-10.3` · [Original reel](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DdHPhLbxg1q/) · 37.746s

**Message:** Differentiate your content by contrasting common advice with what you actually believe.

**Story:** Audience promise → shared-information problem → two-column exercise → contrarian position → useful differentiation.

**Format foundation:** The viewer needs the argument and the speaker's explanation, rather than a second permanent surface. This is a useful contrast to CR07 from the same podcast setting: an exercise can remain conversational when its structure is simple enough to follow.

**Good adaptation:** Preserve eyes, hands and listener reactions. Keep ordinary captions near the face without covering expression. Clear the temporary red promise once the explanation is established. A simple two-column aid is a possible adaptation if the new speech makes the contrast hard to retain; it is not an observed graphic in this reel.

**Limits and counterexample:** Do not infer that mentioning paper requires stock paper footage. Do not put an empty diagram next to every interview. The exercise itself is not demonstrated on a legible sheet here.

**Text roles:** Small white sentence-case speech captions; a larger white opening promise on red backing. Exact font identity is unverified; transfer hierarchy and lifetime, not the red brand treatment.

**Compare:** CR07, LM07. See [Format foundations](../../FORMAT_FOUNDATIONS.md).

**Possible mechanisms:** `native-presenter-crop`, `native-captions`, `titlecard-calm`. These require configuration/composition and review; they are not rendered matches.

## CR04-B04 · 23.5–37.746s

**Message cue (paraphrase):** A different position helps you stand out.

**Visible:** Speaker and listener angles alternate; expressive hands remain visible through the closing explanation.

**Why this format can help:** Conviction and nuance carry the conclusion; an added diagram would need a fresh information job.

**Hold/transition:** End after the retained lesson is stated, preserving the completed gesture and thought.
