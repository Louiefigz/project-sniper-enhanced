# Training pairs — viewer is comparing solutions

The viewer knows there are ways to fix the problem and is choosing between them. The
opening makes the choice, or the deciding factor, concrete.

### T009
Input: "templates are great if you do the same job ten times a month, custom work only
makes sense if every job is actually different"
Output: "Templates or custom? Count how often you repeat the job."
Why it works: turns a vague comparison into a factor the viewer can check.

### T010
Input: "people buy the expensive microphone first, but a cheap lav clipped properly
beats it in a noisy room"
Output: "A clipped-on lav beats the expensive mic in a noisy room."
Why it works: a specific claim from the source that overturns the default purchase.

### T011
Input: "batching sounds efficient but I post more when I record the day I have
something to show"
Output: "Batching felt efficient. Posting on the day worked better."
Why it works: compares two known methods using the speaker's own result.

### T012
Input: "the follow-up that gets answered is the one that asks a yes or no question,
not the one that says just checking in"
Output: "Replace 'just checking in' with one yes-or-no question."
Why it works: names the replacement, not only the problem.

### T013
Input: "you do not need a new tool, you need to write down the three steps you already
repeat and do them in the same order"
Output: "You don't need a new tool. You need your three steps written down."
Why it works: redirects a tool comparison to the actual deciding factor.

### T014
Input: "hiring an editor before you know how you edit just means paying someone to
guess"
Output: "Hire an editor after you've written down how you edit."
Why it works: a prerequisite the viewer can act on before choosing.

### T015
Input: "showing the before and after sold it, explaining the feature never did"
Output: "Stop explaining the feature. Show the before and after."
Why it works: contrasts two approaches with the one that actually worked.

### T016
Input: "the pinned comment brought forty-three subscribers in six days, the paid
promotion brought eleven"
Output: "One pinned comment: 43 subscribers. The paid promotion: 11."
Why it works: a direct comparison with both numbers from the source.
