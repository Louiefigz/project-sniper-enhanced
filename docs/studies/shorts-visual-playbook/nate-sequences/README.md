## How each case is stored

Each beat includes the spoken idea; actual visible objects; what changes; picture function; why that picture supports or weakly matches the words; observed camera action; the inferred reason to move or hold; pacing and continuity; exact catalog candidates; required assets/adaptations; and when to use or avoid it. Full frames sit beside labelled sequences. Each frame records its native index, presentation timestamp and hash. The requested sample time is preserved separately.

The source frames are actual creator pixels, not generated replicas. The frame timestamps are precise; the editorial beat boundaries and speech alignment retain their recorded uncertainty. Some strips intentionally include the preceding state to show a handoff. For typography or small UI labels, inspect the individual full frame rather than reading across thumbnail edges.

## What this evidence establishes

The sample is selected for different storytelling mechanisms, not ranked by watch-time, conversion or creator analytics. It supports more informed planning and gives QA concrete targets. It does not prove these are the best-performing examples, certify a rendered template, or implement automatic reference retrieval. Cases are not whole-video presets and directing beats are not unique templates.

Full chronological half-second context was re-inspected for these cases, with finer inspection of selected motion. Derivative overviews and key source sequences received a delivery check. Source audio was acquired in the broader study but was not auditioned here; exact audio timing, music, sound effects and voice quality remain outside these visual observations.

Keep complete story logic when borrowing a treatment. Reuse the catalog where its information structure and motion fit; obtain the actual evidence assets before production; keep custom work bounded to an identified gap. Judge the assembled Studio result against the same saved references.
