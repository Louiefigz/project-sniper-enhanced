Directing the edit: scope, lanes, style, references

How to get what you want instead of what it guessed, and where its honest limits are.

Scope: how much production you are asking for

There are four levels, and each one includes the one before it:

Ask forYou get
Trim onlyThe base cut, nothing else.
LightThe base cut plus restrained treatment.
ProducedThe full set of production lanes.
FullEverything, every lane on.

The base cut is always on and is not a lane: pause and retake removal, a cold open, bounded reframing, and an audio master at −14 LUFS. You cannot ask for "no editing".

Lanes: the things you switch on and off individually

Each of these is independent, and naming one beats whatever the scope would have implied:

  • Motion — zooms and movement so the frame is not frozen.
  • Graphics — cards, lower thirds, callouts.
  • Transitions — covers for energy cuts and montages.
  • Captions — burned-in subtitles following the kept words.
  • B-roll — supporting footage over the right beats.
  • Credibility — cards backing up a claim or a number.
  • Music bed — ducked under the voice. Off unless you ask.
  • Dialogue cleanup — four strengths; see below.

Say it plainly: "captions and motion on, graphics off". Sniper compares what it planned against what you asked for on every review round, so it cannot quietly downgrade your selection — but it also will not add something you did not request.

Dialogue cleanup, specifically

Ask forWhat it does
"clean up the audio"Cuts rumble, subtracts steady background (room tone, hum, air conditioning), adds presence, evens the dynamics.
"strong audio cleanup"The same, harder. Slightly colours the voice.
"noise-reduction cleanup"Uses a trained speech/noise separator. Better on messy, non-steady noise — traffic, chatter.
"separate the voice from the background" not installed Needs extra software this package does not ship. install/doctor.command reports it as gated. The three above all work.

Brand and style guidance

Sniper ships an original reference library: worked examples of editing mechanisms, each illustrated with frames rendered from its own templates. The editor uses it to match a mechanism to what your words need. Tell it the look in words and it will work inside the render vocabulary it actually has. It picks graphics and transitions from its own catalog by mechanism rather than by name — so "we need a transition here" is a real instruction, not a vague one. You can also hand it a design you made yourself and ask it to animate that.

Using a video you admire as a reference

Point it at a local video or an allowed YouTube, Instagram or TikTok link. A link is downloaded to this Mac with yt-dlp; Chrome cookies are used only if you ask your agent to use them for that one download (see what leaves your Mac). It measures the thing — cut density, motion, transition rate, caption layout, pacing — on this Mac and writes two files: the raw measurements, and a bounded contract derived from them. When you attach the study to an edit, a few of its representative frames go to your provider with the edit's review.

This is guidance, not replication. Measured rhythm can steer an edit. Reproducing a creator's look is not a capability this release claims — the internal qualification cohort for it is 0 of 7. Treat the feature as "measure a rhythm I like and edit in that direction", nothing more.

It will never copy the reference's words, branding, logos, fonts, footage, thumbnails or music. Those are off limits by design, not by policy.

Ordinary inspiration does not trigger any of this. You have to explicitly name a reference.

What it will refuse to do

When Sniper cannot do something it says so and stops. It does not fake it, and it does not quietly deliver less. Most stops are one of:

  • The request needs a choice you have not made (a style, a scope).
  • A requested lane has no asset or no capability to do the job.
  • A claim on screen is not supported by what you actually said.
  • The rendered result failed its own inspection and could not be repaired by a different plan.

Read the message. It names the lane and the reason.
