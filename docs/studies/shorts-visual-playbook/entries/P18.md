## What the reference does

A prompt gives way to several full visual examples. Landscape shots use blurred duplicate fill above/below; a later portrait shot fills the frame. The examples change story/character, so this is a montage of capabilities.

**Type and hierarchy:** Small white/turquoise captions over actual result footage. Most of the frame is reserved for the moving scene; no presenter inset or decorative container.

**Timing:** The sequence shows selected source states; inspect the original interval for exact motion, easing and spoken synchronization.

**Why this treatment helps:** Give the moving output enough space for the viewer to inspect its appearance.

## Apply the mechanism to a new story

**Use when:** The viewer needs to see a moving result, event or story moment.

**Avoid when:** The only available asset is unrelated stock footage.

**Preserve:**

- Use full-frame footage when the imagery is the information.
- Keep subject/project continuity across the selected shots.
- Return to the presenter when interpretation adds value.

**Layout and presenter:** Full prompt for initial instruction; split planning views; full output sequence; split timeline for interpretation.

**Pacing:** Give the connected output sequence several seconds so consistency is visible; do not reduce it to isolated still flashes.

**Return to the person:** After the timeline/output sequence, to explain why directing beats assembling unrelated clips.

**Get these materials first:** Choose one small owned story. Obtain brief, storyboard, character sheet,3–4related output shots and editor timeline. Record selecting one scene and inspecting the corresponding output; avoid swapping to an unrelated example at payoff.

**Improve this limit:** Reference jumps among earthquake, Mighty Mishap, red-haired character and boat/monster examples. Study their presentation mechanics while keeping our proof within one story.

**Do not transfer:**

- Do not overlay an unrelated card over the result.
- Do not use this as proof of character consistency unless the actual sequence supports that claim.
